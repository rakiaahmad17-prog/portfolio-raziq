A price list on a web design site is a promise you cannot keep. "Landing page from Rp 2.500.000" reads like clarity, but the moment a client asks for six sections, a booking form, two rounds of revision and copy in two languages, the number was fiction the whole time. You either eat the difference or you renegotiate, and renegotiating after you have already named a price feels like a bait and switch to the person paying.

So the numbers are not here. What is here is the sequence that produces them.

## The quote comes after the scope, never before

The first conversation is forty-five minutes. You tell me what the business does, who buys from you, and what the current site fails to do. I write it back as a scope: pages, what each page has to accomplish, what has to be built rather than designed, and what you have to supply. That document is where the price is born, because by then both of us are looking at the same list of work.

A number quoted before that list is a guess dressed up as a quote. It is usually wrong in one direction, and the direction it is wrong in is almost never in your favour.

## What actually moves the number

Three things, in order of how much they cost:

1. **How much of the site is built versus arranged.** A five-page site with a treatment finder, a booking flow and a price table is not five pages of work. It is five pages plus three small applications. That is where the budget goes.
2. **Who writes the words.** I can write them, and that is included, but it costs calendar time. If you have a front desk that answers the same nine questions every day, you already have the copy — it is just scattered.
3. **How many people have to approve.** Every additional approver adds a round of revision. Two rounds are in the quote. A third is billed, because it is work.

## The part that is not negotiable

There is a floor. Below it the job cannot be done properly, and a badly built site costs more than no site, because it burns the trust you had already earned in person. If your budget sits under that floor I will say so in the first email rather than in week three, and I will point you at something that fits. That conversation is short and it is better for both of us.

Ask me what you are building. You will get a real number, in writing, with the reasoning attached.
